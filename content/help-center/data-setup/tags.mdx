A tag is a label you attach to a product or a keyword, like `Top Performer` or `cat:HomeCare`. Once tagged, you can filter dashboards, scope Alerts, and group Sherlock signals by that label instead of picking items one by one. An item can carry as many tags as you need.

<Callout type="info">
  To tag products from your connected Seller Central or Vendor Central account, use the **Product Tags** source on the Sources page. You don't need product tracking to tag a product. The **Products** source adds products to tracking and counts against your tracked product limit.
</Callout>

## Why use tags?

Tags pay off as soon as you have more than a handful of products or keywords. With them, you can:

- **Filter dashboards** to a meaningful slice, for example only your `Top Performer` products or only `cat:HomeCare` keywords.
- **Scope Alerts** to a group, so you're pinged when your premium SKUs lose the <Term>Buy Box</Term>, not for every product.
- **Slice ad performance** by your own categories, without restructuring anything in Amazon Seller Central.
- **Get context in Sherlock**: when you triage an anomaly, tags show which part of your catalog is affected.

## Tagging vs tracking

Tagging and tracking are separate. Tagging only labels a product. Tracking makes DataHawk collect that product's page data every day. Pick the right source on the Sources page before you start:

| | Your catalog (connected accounts) | Tracked products |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Your own products, from your Seller Central or Vendor Central account | Products DataHawk monitors daily, yours or competitors' |
| Source to use | **Product Tags** | **Products** |
| Needs product tracking in your plan | No | Yes |
| Counts against your tracked product limit | No | Yes |
| How to tag | **Product Tags** CSV upload | While adding the products, or later with **Product Tags** |

If you only want to group your catalog in dashboards, use **Product Tags**. Track a product only when you need its daily product page data (see [Track Products](/help-center/data-setup/products-tracking)). The same applies to keywords: you can tag keywords you don't track, for example to filter Brand Analytics <Term>SQP</Term> data by your own groups.

### How long until tags show up?

Tags don't apply instantly. DataHawk processes tag uploads every 3 hours, and each run takes about 40 minutes. Your tags reach your data (Snowflake or BigQuery) within **3 hours 40 minutes** at most. Dashboards show them after their next refresh.

## Types of tags

You'll see three kinds of tags in DataHawk. They can sit side by side on the same item.

| Type | Who creates them | When they're attached |
|---|---|---|
| **Automated** | DataHawk | Automatically, based on what DataHawk knows about the item. You can't add or remove them. |
| **Preset** | DataHawk, as a starter list | When you pick one from the suggestions |
| **Custom** | You | Whenever you add or edit a product or keyword |

## Name your tags so they scale

A flat list of tags gets hard to filter after a few dozen. Each tag is a single text value, but you can build structure into the name.

**Add a prefix for each dimension.** Use the `dimension:value` format, so every tag says what it describes:

- Product category: `cat:HomeCare`
- Product group: `pg:Vacuum Cleaner`
- Product color: `color:Red`

Then search `cat:` to see all category tags, `pg:` for all product groups, and so on.

**Use `/` for sub-levels.** Use the `dimension:level1/level2` format:

- `cat:HomeCare/All`
- `cat:HomeCare/Accessory`
- `cat:HomeCare/Vacuum/Cordless`

**Follow a few formatting rules:**

- Always include the prefix (`cat:`, `pg:`, `color:`).
- Keep prefixes lowercase.
- Don't put spaces around `/`.
- Remove leading and trailing spaces.
- Pick a casing convention and stick to it. Tags are case-sensitive: `HomeCare` and `homecare` are two different tags. Lowercase prefixes with PascalCase values (`cat:HomeCare`) work well.

## Manage tags

To manage tags across your workspace, click **View Tags** in the Sources header. From there you can:

- **Add** a tag without attaching it to anything, to set up your naming in advance.
- **Rename** a tag. Every item with the old name follows the rename, with no re-import needed.
- **Remove** a tag from the workspace.

## Where tags show up

Once applied, tags are available across DataHawk:

- **Dashboards (Power BI, Looker Studio):** every standard dashboard has a Tags filter.
- **Alerts:** target items by tag instead of one ASIN at a time.
- **Sherlock:** signals are grouped by tag, so you see patterns across your catalog.
- **Your data (Snowflake, BigQuery):** see [For analysts](#for-analysts-tags-in-your-data-warehouse) below.

Tags belong to one workspace and aren't shared with your other workspaces. If you have several, upload the same tag file to each one.

## FAQ

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="I uploaded tags but I don't see them. What should I check?">

1. **Wait for the next run.** Tags can take up to 3 hours 40 minutes to appear, then dashboards need to refresh.
2. **Check you used the right source.** Tags for your catalog go through **Product Tags**, not **Products**.
3. **Check the product is in your file.** Tags only apply to the exact ASINs listed. If you tagged child ASINs, don't expect the parent to carry the tag.
4. **Check the import mode.** **Replace All** wipes tags that aren't in your file.

Still missing? Email [support@datahawk.co](mailto:support@datahawk.co) with your file and two or three ASINs that should be tagged.

## For analysts: tags in your data warehouse

Product tags live in the `REFERENTIAL` schema, in two tables:

| Table | What it holds | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| `REFERENTIAL.REFERENTIAL_PRODUCT_TAG` | Current product and tag pairs (`PRODUCT_KEY`, `TAG_NAME`), updated every 3 hours | Filter or group data by tag. Power BI dashboards read this table. |
| `REFERENTIAL.DATAHAWK_TAGS_SOURCE` | Each uploaded tag, with `TAG_NAME`, `MARKETPLACE_KEY`, `CHANNEL_PRODUCT_ID`, and `TAG_CREATION_DATE` | Check that a specific upload landed |

To check an upload, count the tags created on the upload date:

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) AS TAGS_CREATED
FROM REFERENTIAL.DATAHAWK_TAGS_SOURCE
WHERE TAG_CREATION_DATE::DATE = '2026-09-24'
```

Replace `COUNT(*) AS TAGS_CREATED` with `TAG_NAME, MARKETPLACE_KEY, CHANNEL_PRODUCT_ID, TAG_CREATION_DATE` to list them instead.

To group your data by tag, join on `PRODUCT_KEY`. The [Google Sheets examples](/help-center/connect-tools/google-sheets) include a full query.
